# Cost-Benefit Analysis: Outsourced vs. In-House Designer

Here's a straightforward cost comparison based on the numbers provided.

## Cost Comparison

| Cost Component | Outsourced Designer | In-House Designer |

## Qualitative Cost-Benefit Analysis

| Factor | Outsourced Designer | In-House Designer |
|---|---|---|
| Total Cost | Lower (**$50K**) | Higher (**$60K**) |
| Flexibility | High; engagement can end after project completion | Lower; ongoing employment commitment |

| Management Overhead | Usually lower | Higher due to employee management |
| Knowledge Retention | Knowledge may leave with vendor | Knowledge remains within organization |

## Recommendation

Based on the costs provided, **outsourcing is the more cost-effective option**.

### Why outsourcing makes sense

- Costs **$50,000** versus **$60,000** for an in-house designer.
- Produces a **$10,000 savings** (approximately **16.7% lower cost**).
- Provides flexibility if design needs decrease after the initial project.
- Avoids recruiting, onboarding, and employment-related overhead.

### When an in-house designer may be better

- Design work is expected to continue beyond the six-month project.
- Product design and user experience are strategic capabilities for the company.
- Close, daily collaboration with developers is important.
- Long-term ownership and retention of design knowledge are priorities.

## Final Suggestion

For the **six-month time horizon described**, I recommend **outsourcing**. The outsourced option delivers the required design capability at a lower total cost (**$50,000 vs. $60,000**) while maintaining flexibility and reducing long-term commitment.
